untype
hold a key, speak, release.

A Swift-native push-to-talk dictation tool for the macOS menu bar. It streams speech to a realtime STT provider, types it live into whatever you have focused, and lets an LLM clean it, translate it or answer it.

untype · technical deck
01
What untype is
A single-user macOS tool for dictating into any application, with a voice protocol designed for talking to coding agents.
Section 01
01 · The problem
Typing is the slowest part of working with an agent.

Prompts, commands and messages are spoken in seconds but typed in minutes. Dictation built into the OS lands raw, unpunctuated text and never reaches the field you are actually working in.

  • Raw transcripts carry filler words, false starts and grammar noise.
  • Greek and English are mixed in daily work, and the target often needs the other language.
  • Text should land at the caret of the frontmost app, not in a separate window.
  • A coding agent needs a way to hear "send", "cancel" and "status" without a mouse.
Section 01
01 · The product
Hold a hotkey, speak, release. Clean text appears at the cursor.
01

Push-to-talk

A global hotkey (default Control+`) opens the audio gate while held; release finalizes the turn and delivers the output. Push-to-listen mode toggles instead: press to start, press again to stop.

02

Realtime STT

Microphone audio streams over WebSocket to Soniox or ElevenLabs. Partials render live; finals drive the protocol.

03

Refine, translate or ask

An LLM cleans disfluencies, translates between Greek and English, or, with Ask on, answers what you said. Selected text and the previous answer can go along.

04

Live typing at the caret

While you speak, the words are typed straight into the focused control. On release they are replaced in place by the processed text, and optionally copied to the clipboard.

Two things are treated as non-negotiable: push-to-talk must always work, and processed text must always reach the focused input. Every change is verified against both.
Section 01
01 · Who it serves
One person, one Mac, every app.
Typical targets
  • Coding agents and chat UIs in the browser or terminal.
  • Editors, Notes, Outlook and other rich-text bodies.
  • Any macOS control that accepts text through Accessibility or paste.
Bilingual by design
  • Default language hints el,en; Greek script detection selects the source language.
  • Translation policies opposite, to-en, to-el.
  • The default guard phrase is Greek: τέλος εντολής.
Two front ends
  • The app bundle: a menu-bar icon with a dropdown, a Transcript window, a Settings window, the overlay and the hotkey.
  • untype [options]: terminal streaming with the same runtime.
Voice-agent protocol
  • Spoken markers such as command send, command cancel or command ask on control sections and operators.
  • Machine-readable JSONL events let an agent consume the dictation stream.

Section 01
01 · Principles
Three rules shape every decision in the codebase.
01

No fallbacks for configuration

A missing required value raises a typed missing_configuration error and exits with code 2. The tool never invents a key, an endpoint or a model.

02

Privacy-safe diagnostics

Logs and events never contain transcript text, processed output, clipboard contents, prompts or secrets. Only names, states, codes and timings.

03

Guarded core features

Push-to-talk and focused-input delivery are protected by explicit guardrails, per-release manual smoke checks, and a latency log that proves delivery worked.

Consequence for operators: most first-run problems are configuration or permission errors that the tool reports precisely, rather than silent degradations.

How it works
From the key press to the caret: audio capture, streaming transcription, the voice protocol, LLM processing and delivery.
Section 02
02 · Pipeline
Six stages, one in-process runtime.
1 · Trigger

Hotkey press and release

Quartz event tap with Carbon and NSEvent fallbacks; the audio gate opens on press.

UntypeHotkeyMonitor · HotkeySessionControl
2 · Capture

Microphone to PCM16

AVAudioEngine tap, mono 16-bit at the configured rate, silence while the gate is closed.

AVFoundationAudioSource · PCM16MonoConverter
3 · Transcribe

Realtime STT stream

WebSocket to Soniox or ElevenLabs; partials and finals flow back to the runtime.

SonioxTranscriber · ElevenLabsTranscriber
4 · Protocol

Markers and sections

Finals are parsed for spoken commands; text is buffered into sections with operator flags.

VoiceAgentProtocolController · VoiceCommandStateMachine
5 · LLM

Refine, translate, ask

One composite call, or one Ask call with selection and conversation; fail-open on error.

AzureOpenAIRefiner · GoogleRefiner · AnthropicMessagesRefiner
6 · Deliver

Live typing, then in place

Typed live while held, edited in place on release; AX, paste or key events otherwise.

LiveFocusedInputTyper · FocusedInputDelivery
Each stage is timed into one record of release-latency.jsonl. The whole chain is assembled by UntypeRuntimeFactory into a single TranscriptionSessionRuntime, shared by the CLI and the native UI.
Section 02
02 · Push-to-talk
The lifecycle of one press.
1

Warm session

Enabling the hotkey starts a hotkey-owned runtime with the gate closed. Idle sessions are recycled every 5 minutes; a session that dies on its own, for example when the provider closes an idle socket, is re-warmed with backoff.

2

Press

The target app and any selected text are captured, the overlay appears, the gate opens and the words start being typed live. A press during the previous delivery is queued, not lost. In push-to-listen mode the first press starts and the next one stops.

3

Release

The gate closes, the provider is asked to commit, and the runtime waits up to 1.5 s for final text. Quick Close submits the latest partial immediately instead.

4

Submit and restart

The LLM runs while the overlay shows a processing wave, the live text is replaced in place, "this turn" operators switch off, and a fresh warm session starts.

Section 02
02 · Hotkey detection
Three detection layers share one press/release state.
Primary

Quartz CGEvent tap

System-wide key down and key up, with suppression of the hotkey so it never reaches the frontmost app. Needs Accessibility and Input Monitoring.

Status: global event tap ready

Fallback 1

Carbon RegisterEventHotKey

A global hotkey registration that fires reliably even when the event tap cannot be installed.

Status: carbon global hotkey ready; event tap unavailable

Fallback 2

AppKit NSEvent monitors

Local and global monitors as the last resort; release may be blocked, so the UI asks the user to press again.

Status: fallback monitor active

The hotkey monitor is reconfigured only when the hotkey string or its enabled flag changes, so a settings write mid-press can no longer reset a live session. A UI "Push to Talk" button drives the same path when keyboard hooks are unavailable.
Section 02
02 · Audio capture
Microphone first, provider second.
Capture
  • AVFoundationAudioSource preflights microphone permission and installs an AVAudioEngine input tap with 1 024-frame buffers.
  • PCM16MonoConverter downmixes to mono 16-bit PCM at the configured sample rate (default 16 000 Hz).
  • Errors are typed: microphone_permission exits with code 3, microphone_capture for engine failures.
Gating and forwarding
  • The runtime starts the microphone before connecting the provider socket, so the UI shows audio activity early.
  • While the gate is closed, silence is substituted for every chunk; the provider connection stays warm.
  • An AudioActivitySnapshot (waiting, silent, active, muted) is emitted every 0.25 s for the waveform and status pill.
Diagnostics trace the order explicitly: starting microphone capture, microphone capture started, connecting realtime stream, realtime stream connected.
Section 02
02 · Speech-to-text providers
Two realtime providers behind one RuntimeTranscriber protocol.
Soniox · default
  • Model stt-rt-v5 (v4 is an alias) at wss://stt-rt.soniox.com/transcribe-websocket.
  • Startup frame carries the key, pcm_s16le, sample rate, endpoint detection, language hints or auto identification, and optional context text from prompt file 004.
  • Release sends {"type":"finalize"} followed by a 250 ms drain. Utterance boundaries arrive as <end> and <fin> tokens.
  • Error frames map 401/403 to soniox_auth, 503 to soniox_network, the rest to soniox_protocol.
ElevenLabs
  • Model scribe_v2_realtime at wss://api.elevenlabs.io/v1/speech-to-text/realtime.
  • Query carries model_id, audio_format=pcm_<rate>, commit strategy, optional language code and keyterms from prompt file 006.
  • Audio is sent as base64 input_audio_chunk messages; previous_text from file 005 goes with the first chunk only.
  • Allowed sample rates: 8 000, 16 000, 22 050, 24 000, 44 100, 48 000 Hz.
Both adapters sit behind a mockable RealtimeWebSocketClient and are frame-level tested; live behaviour is covered by manual smoke procedures under test_scripts/.
Section 02
02 · Voice-agent protocol
Markers turn speech into sections; sections carry operators.
MarkerA spoken phrase recognised in a final transcript. Four kinds: state_command, section_end, section_cancel, literal_next. Matching is case and diacritic insensitive with Greek locale rules and requires word boundaries.
Default phrasesCommand phrase command, section end command send, section cancel command cancel, literal next literal phrase. The guard phrase τέλος εντολής is an alias of section end.
SectionThe dictated text buffered between markers, identified as sec_000001 and so on. "Command send" submits it with the active operators; "command cancel" discards it; shutdown submits pending text.
Interaction modesdictation (default), agent-protocol (JSONL events on stdout, no human status lines) and hybrid (both; the CLI requires --protocol-output).
EventsProtocol untype.voice-agent.v1: session started and ended, state and status, section submitted, processed and cancelled, streaming progress, clipboard copied, input sent, warnings. Every event has a monotonic seq.
Section 02
02 · Operators and voice commands
Seven switches decide what happens to a turn.
OperatorKeyEffectDefault lifetime
refineRThe LLM cleans the transcript, preserving meaning and language.Until changed
translateTTranslate to the target (opposite, English or Greek) set next to the switch.Until changed
askAWhat you say is a request to the model; its answer replaces the dictation.This turn
followupFAsk also sends the conversation so far, so the model can revise its last answer.This turn
selectionSThe text selected in the app in front goes along, as material or as context.This turn
clipboardCCopy the result to the clipboard.Until changed
inputIType the result into the focused control of the frontmost app.Until changed
Toggle an operator in the menu-bar dropdown, in Settings, with the hotkey plus its letter while recording, or by voice: command ask on. Settings decides per operator whether a change lasts this turn or until changed; flags such as --ask-default set the starting state. An operator that needs an unconfigured LLM delivers the text unprocessed, with a warning.
Section 02
02 · Ask, Follow-up and Selection
Talk to the model, not just through it.
A · Ask

Dictate a request, get the answer

  • "Write a short reply thanking them and proposing Tuesday." The answer is typed where the request was.
  • One call with prompts 010 and 011; with Translate on, prompt 012 writes the answer in the target language.
  • Refine is skipped. Own limits: 8 000 output tokens and a 120 s timeout.
F · Follow-up

Revise the previous answer

  • With Follow-up on, Ask sends the earlier requests and answers too: "shorter", "more formal", "add a closing line".
  • Keeps the last 20 exchanges in memory only, never on disk.
  • Off means a new conversation; the dropdown also has New conversation.
S · Selection

Work on the selected text

  • Read through Accessibility at press time, or by a ⌘C that restores your clipboard.
  • Material (default): the selection is processed and replaced; a silent tap is enough.
  • Context: reference only; the result goes after the selection.
Combine them. Select a paragraph, turn on Selection and Translate, tap the hotkey: the paragraph is replaced by its translation. Select an email, turn on Ask and Selection, say "draft a polite no": the draft appears in place.
Section 02
02 · LLM refinement
Clean the transcript, never answer it.

The refinement system prompt describes the input as a verbatim transcript with disfluencies and asks for a grammatically clean rewrite that keeps the speaker's meaning and language.

  • The text is content to clean, never a message to the model: questions are not answered, instructions are not followed, and nothing is added.
  • The output stays in the input language. A request for translation inside the transcript is part of the text, not a command.
  • Providers implemented: Azure OpenAI, Google Gemini, and Claude, either direct (anthropic) or on Microsoft Foundry (azure-anthropic). Six other names are accepted but raise a configuration error if refinement is on.
  • Temperature 0.2 for OpenAI and Gemini; Claude uses effort low instead. Fixed 15 s timeout and one keep-alive URLSession, saving 100 to 400 ms per release.
Section 02
02 · Translation
Greek or English in, the other language out.
Language detection

Any character in the Greek and Greek Extended Unicode blocks marks the section as Greek; otherwise it is English. Only these two protocol languages exist.

Policies
  • opposite (default): Greek becomes English, English becomes Greek.
  • to-en: always English.
  • to-el: always Greek.
Prompts

System prompt from file 002, user template from file 003 with {target_language} and {text}. Technical terms, file names, command names and code identifiers are preserved verbatim.

Translation only runs for sections whose translate operator is on. With the operator off, the refinement prompt explicitly forbids any language change, so refine-only sections can never come back translated.
Section 02
02 · Composite refine and translate
Both operators on means one LLM round trip.

When a section has refine and translate enabled and a composite processor is configured, the controller sends a single request whose system prompt (file 007) demands strict JSON with two fields.

  • The user message stacks the source transcript, the refinement instruction (file 008) and the translation instruction (file 009).
  • The response is parsed strictly; the JSON object is extracted even if the model wraps it.
  • There is no sequential fallback: a composite failure is a fail-open warning and the raw text is delivered.
  • Known gap: the same elapsed time is recorded in both refine_ms and translate_ms.
// system prompt: respond with ONLY valid JSON
{
  "refined_text":    "...",   // original language
  "translated_text": "..."    // target language
}

// user message envelope (LLMRefiners.swift)
Source transcript:
{raw text}

Refinement prompt:
{file 008 with {text} filled}

Translation prompt:
{file 009 with {target_language} filled}

Return only JSON with refined_text and translated_text.
Section 02
02 · The overlay
A small oscilloscope, not a text box.
  • While the hotkey is held, a transparent 200×84 pt panel at the bottom centre of the screen draws the real microphone signal, with automatic gain so quiet speech still swings.
  • Under the wave, seven chips R T A F S C I show the operators: solid amber when on. Press the hotkey plus a letter to flip one mid-turn.
  • After release a breathing wave stays on screen while the model refines, translates or answers, and the panel hides once the text has been delivered.
  • The panel never takes focus, ignores the mouse, joins every Space and is recreated at every appearance.
  • The transcript is not in the overlay any more: it is typed into your field. --llm-streaming remains, but with nothing to display it only matters for logs.

02 · Delivery
Reaching the caret: live first, three fallbacks.
0

Live typing while you speak

With Input on, the transcript is typed into the app that was in front at press time. On release only the differing tail is deleted and retyped; a result with a line break is pasted. These keystrokes carry a marker the hotkey monitors ignore.

1

AX caret insertion

For turns without live text: set kAXSelectedText at the caret and verify the length changed, which protects rich-text bodies such as Outlook.

2

Clipboard-preserving paste

Snapshot the pasteboard, paste with ⌘V, restore the clipboard after 0.8 s. Browsers get this first.

3

Unicode key events

Type the text as key events when it is at most 500 UTF-16 units.

Do not move the caret or type while holding the hotkey: live text is edited relative to where it started. The app delivers in-process, so the grant belongs to untype.app; CLI runs use untype-input-helper.

What to read

  • outcome tells whether text reached the field, was processed without it, or failed.

  • Per-stage durations point at the slow link: provider wait, LLM, or delivery.
  • Opt-in via UNTYPE_RELEASE_LATENCY_LOG; never contains transcript text.

Section 03
03 · Signing and notarization
Why the signature is a functional requirement, not a formality.

macOS ties Accessibility and Input Monitoring grants to the app's code-signing designated requirement. An ad-hoc signature changes on every build, so each redeploy silently revokes Accessibility and delivery fails with accessibility_not_trusted.

  • Since build 7 every deploy is signed with the Developer ID Application identity and notarized.
  • Grants survive upgrades as long as the same certificate signs the app.
  • Grants are lost when the identity changes: certificate renewal, switching certificate type, or an ad-hoc build. The bundle id must also stay fixed.
  • The main binary is the bundle executable itself, with no launcher, so the process installing the event tap matches the identity granted in System Settings.
Certificate dates to plan around: Developer ID expires 2027-02-01, Apple Development expires 2027-08-15. Each renewal costs one permission re-grant.

Section 03
03 · macOS permissions, step by step
Where to click in System Settings.
1 · Microphone
  • Click Start Listening (or hold the hotkey) once. macOS asks "untype would like to access the microphone": click Allow.
  • No prompt, or Don't Allow clicked earlier? System Settings › Privacy & Security › Microphone, switch untype on.
  • untype appears in that list only after it has asked once, so do the first step first.
2 · Accessibility
  • System Settings › Privacy & Security › Accessibility.
  • Click + below the list, pick Applications › untype, click Open, make sure the switch next to untype is on. macOS may ask for your password or Touch ID.
  • Quit untype and open it again.
  • Switch on but still "not trusted" (typical after an update changed the signature)? Select untype, click −, add it again with +, quit and reopen.
3 · Input Monitoring
  • Only if the hotkey does not fire while another app is in front.
  • System Settings › Privacy & Security › Input Monitoring.
  • Click +, pick Applications › untype, click Open, switch it on. An older untype entry there? Remove it with − first.
  • Quit untype and open it again.
The welcome screen shows this guide with an Open … settings button per pane and a Re-check status button; the status is read live from macOS. Each pane lists untype only after it has been added there once, and every change needs a quit and relaunch.
Section 03
03 · Recovering lost grants
When delivery stops after a redeploy or certificate change.

Symptom

The latency log shows outcome: focused_input_failed with code: accessibility_not_trusted, or the hotkey stops firing after an install.

Cause

The grant is tied to an older signing identity or bundle id.

# reset ONLY the Accessibility grant for the app
tccutil reset Accessibility com.local.untype

# relaunch, then re-add /Applications/untype.app under
# System Settings → Privacy & Security → Accessibility
open -a /Applications/untype.app

# hotkey dead after install? remove the stale entry under
# Input Monitoring, re-add the app, relaunch
Never reset Input Monitoring when only Accessibility is broken. That removes the grant the event tap depends on and breaks push-to-talk, which then needs its own re-grant.
Section 03
03 · Provider credentials
Setting up the API keys.
In the app

Welcome screen › Set keys…, or Settings › General › Session › Edit keys…

  • Speech-to-text, one required: Soniox API key (the default provider) or ElevenLabs API key (with the provider switched to ElevenLabs).
  • LLM, only for Refine, Translate or Ask: the Azure OpenAI key, endpoint, deployment and API version; or GOOGLE_API_KEY; or Claude: ANTHROPIC_API_KEY direct, or ANTHROPIC_FOUNDRY_API_KEY with ANTHROPIC_FOUNDRY_RESOURCE on Microsoft Foundry.
  • Secrets are masked; a Show switch reveals them while editing. Save writes only the fields you changed; an emptied field unsets that key.
  • Keys are stored in ~/.tool-agents/untype/.env, readable only by you, and never appear in logs or diagnostics.
In the file

Edit ~/.tool-agents/untype/.env directly

  • The first session start creates it as a fully commented template: every variable as # NAME= with its description, allowed values and default.
  • Remove the leading # and fill the value, for example SONIOX_API_KEY=… or AZURE_OPENAI_API_KEY=…; comments and order are preserved, and later duplicates of a key are removed by the editor.
  • Optional expiry dates, SONIOX_API_KEY_EXPIRES_AT and ELEVENLABS_API_KEY_EXPIRES_AT, make the UI warn 14 days before a key expires.
  • Settings › General › Credentials shows each key's name, source tier and expiry, never the value. Pick the LLM provider and model in Settings › Refinement (LLM).
Nothing is defaulted. A required key that is missing everywhere is a configuration error that names the variable and the four places it may be set; the onboarding checklist stays open until a speech-to-text key is present.

Configuration and moving parts
Where every setting lives, how the four tiers resolve, and what the state, prompt and log files contain.
Section 04
04 · Resolution chain
Four tiers, highest priority first, and no invented values.
Tier 1 · highest

CLI flag

Explicit arguments such as --stt-provider. UI settings are converted into the equivalent flags, so what the UI sets behaves like a flag.

Tier 2

Local .env

The .env file in the current working directory, useful for per-project overrides when running the CLI.

Tier 3

User .env

~/.tool-agents/untype/.env: the normal home of API keys and provider settings for the app bundle. Provisioned as a commented template on first start; the UI credentials editor writes here.

Tier 4 · lowest

Shell environment

Exported variables of the launching shell. The UI shows which tier supplied the API key.

Parser rules

Comments and blank lines skipped, export prefix stripped, quotes handled, inline comments removed, last duplicate wins, blank values fall through to the next tier. Booleans accept true/false, yes/no, on/off, 1/0.

No-fallback rule

A required value that is missing everywhere raises a typed error naming the variable and the four places it may be set. Nothing is defaulted silently, and startup stops with exit code 2.

04 · Where the moving parts live
Everything user-specific sits in one folder.

04 · Customising prompts
Edit, save, and the next turn already uses it.
  • Settings › Prompts lists the files by group: Refinement, Translation, Refine + translate, Ask, Selected text, Transcription context.
  • Edit, then Save (⌘S). Revert drops unsaved edits; Reset to Default loads the built-in text for you to save.
  • A save is validated with the same rules as session start, so a missing placeholder cannot be written.
  • untype checks the files every second, including edits from another editor. The Events tab shows prompts.reloaded and an idle warm session restarts at once; a turn in progress is never interrupted.
  • Keep two invariants: refinement must not change the language, and only Ask may answer the dictated text.

Section 05
05 · Daily workflow
Focus a field, hold, speak, release.
01

Set the turn

In the menu-bar dropdown, switch on what this turn needs: Ask, Follow-up or Selection. Refine, Translate, Clipboard and Input usually stay on.

02

Focus and hold

Click into the target field, or select the text to work on. Hold the hotkey, or press once in push-to-listen mode, and speak. Your words appear as you say them.

03

Flip mid-turn

Still holding, press R, T, A, F, S, C or I to switch an operator. The overlay chips show the result. Keep the caret where it is.

04

Release

The wave breathes while the model works, then the live text is replaced in place. Operators set to this turn switch off again.

Quick Close trades accuracy for speed: it submits the latest partial the moment you release. In Agent or Hybrid mode, say "command send", "command cancel", "command status" or "command ask on".

Section 05
05 · Guardrails for contributors
Two features may never regress.

Do not break push-to-talk.

Any change to the UI, session controls, hotkey configuration, permission flow, overlay or runtime lifecycle must keep the whole chain: listening stays active, press and release reach the hotkey-owned session, the gate opens and closes, the overlay shows and hides, and delivery runs after release. Verify in NativeUntypeUILauncher, UntypeHotkeyMonitor, HotkeySessionControl and TranscriptionSessionRuntime.

Do not break focused-input delivery.

Any change touching delivery, live typing, the protocol controller, the packaging script, or any redeploy must be followed by a real release into a text field with focused_input.ok=true in the log. untype's own keystrokes must keep the marker that the hotkey monitors ignore.

Never redeploy an ad-hoc bundle.

Always package with the Developer ID identity and notarize; an unsigned build silently revokes Accessibility.
